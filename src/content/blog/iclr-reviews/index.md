Today was mostly tiring, so I'm going to keep this one short.

What's mostly been happening these days is the preparing to rebuttal for the ICLR reviews from one of my unpublished papers. One of the reviews was asking for empirical evidence for a quantitative claim and for showing how this work contributes to a problem that was not previously answered by similar works in the field.

We drafted up our responses by chalking down the differences between these two works: showing in elaborate detail what the other one was doing, what ours is presenting, and how it is specifically different from what the previous works were not able to show. We also gave numbers, details of the experiments, and empirical evidence of how our work contributes to a better result.

However the response of the reviewer was to double down on his question. While they understand the workflows we support, the numbers we are contributing, and the experiments we have conducted, they are still finding it hard to understand how our work is relevant and how we are solving a problem that the other papers are not solving. Apparently the empirical evidence is not enough.

I really don't understand how to make an empirical claim without relying on the empirical evidence. This is a problem I'm currently solving right now while I brainstorm for the second rebuttal. Till then I hope I sleep well tonight and wake up refreshed tomorrow so that I can give you the true AI safety blog that you deserve. See you tomorrow.
