Today for my daily blog (a habit I’m cultivating where I try to write daily about a technical or AI Safety-adjacent topic I read about. It’s on my personal website.), I tried reading Nick Bostrom’s book “Superintelligence” and its just really boring for some reason. Or maybe I have this bias in my head because this is a book that foundationalized AI Safety but it came out in like 2014 (sure there were revisions till 2017), so it already feels like an ancient take on a recent problem. The exponentially rising pace of AI Safety makes even fairly recent progress feel extinct already. I’m not kidding - a lot of work from 2025 and even early 2026 is already irrelevant to the sheer stage of progress in AI Safety today. Anyways, the only chapter I read seemed to talk about whether the early trailblazers of the field will have a huge strategic advantage over the laggers, those lagging; how this would be affected by the speed of AI development, and what conditions this advantage rests on.


I would have read it all, explained it here and deduced some smart conclusions about the AGI race we see currently and whether trailblazers actually had a strategic advantage over the laggers or not. But cut me some slack - I'm bored and tired. I had too many Diwali sweets, I've mentally regressed and am consuming junk I gave up on long ago, and this unhealthy appetite is eating up my aptitude. So I'm realizing I don't have to write about these ideas from Chapter 5 if I don't want to! (Here's what it was for those curious: Nick writes about an interesting exploration of technologies and flagbearers in fields like silk production by the Chinese for 2 millennia, porcelain making not reaching Europe until sixteenth century, and how medium and slow speed AI growth could have multiple competitors at comparative speeds of innovation, with equal probability of market domination but they could also be hit by anti-monopoly laws, governmental and societal pressure, and how much AI is part of the trailblazing development. So yeah that's it). I'll talk about our increasing existential crises instead :)

Casually chatting with my wonderfully smart WE friends, we quickly stumble on the concept of the meaning of life (as girls usually do), and what the average person looks forward to from our generation as compared to our parents. Our older generations seemed to plan their life to not only make comfort for their offsprings, but also for their offsprings' marriage and eventual offsprings. But for our generation, its so hard to imagine a life of stability that involves either buying a house or a car, let alone marriage or kids or the sweet embrace of retirement. While pondering, I pulled up the numbers on how different financial goals are for GenZ than for all generations before them. Most of the numbers I mention will be pulled from [1]. I was shocked to read how the majority of the generation (26%) still dreamt of buying their own homes. This is our top financial goal - sweet innocent GenZ's. After this comes the financial goal of saving up for an emergency fund, then comes the goal of travel, and finally thinking about retirement savings, which happens to be the primary goal for Generation X. Despite wanting to buy homes, 29% of GenZ (Americans) have completely given up on this pipe dream simply because of the exorbitant prices, rising cost of livelihood, higher median salaries than our ancestors but also higher median income needed to now buy a house (6 years needed now, instead of the meagre 3.5 years needed in 1975) [2]. All perfectly reasonable choices.

Stumbling on the numbers, I also saw that 87% of GenZ choose to save or invest their money rather than spend, unfortunately damaging our public perception of being the most reckless generation known to mankind. The YouGov survey also found that 65% of adults had already started investing by the time they turned 30. Not GenZ, but good numbers still: 39% Indians began between the ages of 18 and 25. 

The numbers sing the same unanimous chorus we all reach from time to time: GenZ is a deeply optimistic yet pragmatic generation stuck in a time where its practical to not sit on your rainbow of hope. AQIs cross 2000, governments collude with big tech billionaires to lie about the future of the economy, jobs feel uncertain, houses can't be bought, healthy food is expensive, crime rates are rising and we are further apart from each other than ever before despite having access to every minute detail of each other's lives. So yes we invest for safety, but we also spend on travel, and we spend on good food, and we make silly reels and dance to silly songs as most of us rally up to protest against our governments all over the world somehow simultaneously on widely different but wildly connected issues, and we don't take things seriously, and we anticipate the collapse of all we know, and we still go to work, and we prepare for the worst and save up food and water for months in our garages (you know, just in case), and we give up on the idea of a house, marriage, kids, a career ladder and retirement and we drink expensive drinks and we go out, and we take supplements, and we work out, and we do wacky hobbies and encourage each other to do whatever feels good. The ceiling of our unstructured world holds within itself the chaotic structure that most of us try to cultivate in our lives. Simply because its the only thing we can do. So we do it. While it feels unfair to have to live in a world where we did not sow the seeds or reap the benefits, but can only bear the cumulative losses of everyone's existence; we are done blaming our ancestors (who got a headstart) and are instead ready for answers. We are more than willing to join hands with everyone else, disadvantage or not, to figure out how we can start cleaning this mess.

So I guess no, trailblazers did not have a strategic advantage in this case, because they still have to shake hands with us laggers to brainstorm the solution.
